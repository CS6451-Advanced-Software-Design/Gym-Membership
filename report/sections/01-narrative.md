We have built a membership system for a gym because right now, the staff at the front desk of the gym/fitness club are doing most of the work by hand. The gym sells 4 types of plans. After joining, the member pays for the first month and gets a 7-day free trial. If the payment is completed, the member becomes Active after the 7-day trial; if not, the membership becomes Lapsed. The membership can also be paused (frozen) for 1–3 months, once a year, and the member doesn't pay while the membership is frozen. If a payment fails 2 times in a row, the membership is Lapsed and the member can't use the gym facilities or book classes until he/she pays what's owed. If the membership stays Lapsed for 60 days, it is cancelled. Also, different plans have some rules: the Annual membership fee is paid at once, comes with no free trial and costs €50 to cancel in the first 6 months; the Student plan needs a student ID; and the Off-Peak plan only allows weekday timings between 10:00 and 16:00.

There are some more rules which classes and billing introduce. The capacity of classes is fixed, and after a class gets full, members get an entry on a first-come, first-served waitlist in case someone cancels. If they miss 3 classes in 30 days, they will not be able to book for a week. Each month, every member's bill is worked out from their plan price, so the bill is the plan price minus the best discount for their category [corporate (15%), family (10%) or loyalty (5%)], which don't stack. Apart from this, any penalties and charges for premium classes are also added, and €20 is taken off for each friend referral, only up to €60 a year. Because of all these rules, the system calculates every answer from the member's plan, status, history and the date. So, this is a proper working backend system, not just data being shown from a database, and it helps the front desk with tasks they do by hand. We've built the backend for four main areas: Membership, Booking, Member check-in and Billing. It's used through a REST API, with Postman or the Swagger UI instead of a real front end and JSON files instead of a real database.
